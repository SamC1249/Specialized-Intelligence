# Adversarial-Agent Plan — 2026-11-11

> Context: the last concrete plan is `docs/plan-2026-06-20.md`
> (seed / bootstrap). Between then and now, `Coding-Agent` shipped a
> working skeleton: four offline adapters (Wikimedia, Internet Archive,
> PeerTube, Common Crawl JSON-LD), metadata-only quality scorer, compare
> harness + CLI, fixture-driven e2e test, and a GitHub Actions CI matrix
> on Python 3.11/3.12 with `pre-commit`. Baseline:
> `reports/baseline-2026-06-20.json` — `mean_quality` wikimedia 0.62 >
> peertube 0.57 > common_crawl 0.53 > archive_org 0.36, with only 8
> fixture records total.
>
> This plan **attacks that skeleton**. The brittle parts are the ones
> the baseline is mute about: tiny N, no cross-source dedup, no
> multilingual coverage, no license-laundering defense, no procedural
> signal beyond `has_steps`, and CI that never exercises a realistic
> record volume.

---

## Mission restatement (and why this matters beyond cooking)

Find **internet-scale, legally clean** video data for frontier video
world models. Cooking is only the *stress test*: the real contract is
"given an arbitrary difficult video domain (surgery, lab protocols,
manufacturing assembly, sports plays), can our pipeline enumerate,
license-verify, deduplicate, procedurally-segment, and benchmark
sources without any paid data and without ToS violations?"

Any component that is cooking-specific must be flagged as such and have
a `domain=` extension point.

## Hypotheses under test today

> **H1 (yield ceiling).** Our current four-source allowlist is
> *cooking-biased* and *speech-heavy-biased*. Adding (a) the Web Data
> Commons **schema.org Recipe** corpus (5.68M URLs in the 2023 release)
> as a pre-crawled index over Common Crawl, and (b) a
> **Wikimedia-Commons-category** adapter that recurses
> `Category:Videos of cooking` and `Category:Videos of cooking
> recipes`, lifts recall of unique, license-clean procedural videos by
> ≥5× on an unbiased English evaluation set. We measure this with a
> `yield` benchmark column, not just `n_records`.

> **H2 (license-laundering defense).** Trusting the per-video license
> field reported by any single source is dangerous. The Common Pile
> (arXiv 2506.05209) explicitly dropped YouTube Commons for this
> reason. We should ship a **two-of-two license agreement** rule:
> a record is `license_verified=True` only if (i) the API response
> declares a redistributable license **and** (ii) an independent
> re-fetch of the landing page or license page confirms the same
> license string. Records that disagree are demoted to `UNKNOWN`,
> never deleted. We predict this will reclassify 5–15% of current
> fixture "clean" records to `UNKNOWN`, and that number itself is the
> benchmark we want to track over time.

> **H3 (near-duplicate inflation).** The same cooking video is often
> re-hosted on Internet Archive, PeerTube instances, and recipe blogs
> (Common Crawl). The baseline treats these as independent yield.
> Cross-source perceptual dedup — even just a title+duration MinHash
> prefilter, before any frame-level pHash — will reduce effective
> corpus size by 10–30% while *improving* mean_quality (because the
> higher-quality copy per cluster wins).

> **H4 (metadata-only quality is leaky).** `_score_text_density` and
> `_score_duration` are gameable: a 5-minute recipe blog spam page
> with a template description scores high. A **language detection
> gate** on title+description plus a **domain-reputation score**
> (Tranco / Majestic top-1M bucket, but open-source only — e.g.
> Tranco list under CC-BY 4.0) will down-rank spam and up-rank
> known-good recipe hosts, without fetching any video bytes.

> **H5 (CI silently rots).** Today's e2e test only validates that the
> harness runs on 8 fixture rows. There is no (a) schema-regression
> test (`db_structured.md` vs `records.py` drift), (b) license
> regression test (every allowlisted source produces at least one
> record with `license.is_redistributable == True`), (c) baseline
> comparison (today's harness output vs `reports/baseline-*.json`
> within a tolerance), or (d) adversarial fixture for the Common
> Crawl JSON-LD parser (nested `@graph`, `Recipe` with embedded
> `VideoObject`, malformed `@context`, laundered license URL). We
> are blind to 80% of the ways this pipeline can silently degrade.

## Why the frontier-video angle still points at cooking

Three independent 2025–2026 data points, from today's research:

1. The **Common Pile v0.1** (arXiv 2506.05209) demonstrates that the
   *cleanest* way to get at YouTube CC-BY content is **channel-level
   curation** — not per-video license trust. 2,000 channels → 1.1M
   videos / 470,000 hours of speech. The analogous move for cooking
   is a curated **"legally-clean cooking creators" allowlist** we
   maintain in-repo and version. Notes in
   `docs/artifacts/2026-11-11-common-pile.md`.
2. **Web Data Commons** continues to extract schema.org from Common
   Crawl. The 2023-12 release has 5.68M `schema.org/Recipe` instances
   across 4.49M URLs and 42,727 hosts, downloadable as N-Quads with
   source-page URLs. This is a **pre-built index** we should read
   instead of re-walking Common Crawl HTML ourselves. Notes in
   `docs/artifacts/2026-11-11-web-data-commons.md`.
3. Procedural segmentation literature (CROT ICASSP'25, HVQ 2024,
   OTAS WACV'24, Tasty/YouCook2 Closer-Look BMVC'22) all agree that
   step-boundary signal is *the* downstream quality axis for cooking
   world models. We cannot compute it from metadata, but we can
   compute a **"procedural-ness prior"** from the number of
   `recipe_steps`, average step length, and verb density in step
   text. Notes in `docs/artifacts/2026-11-11-procedural-segmentation.md`.

## Concrete deliverables for `Coding-Agent` (prioritized)

### P0 — schema & test hardening (do these first; everything else depends on them)

- [ ] `tests/test_schema_contract.py`: parse `db_structured.md` field
      tables and assert that `VideoRecord`, `Provenance`, `SourceQuery`,
      `BenchmarkResult` fields and types exactly match. Fail CI on
      drift. This is the single most under-tested invariant in the repo.
- [ ] `tests/test_license_floor.py`: for every registered source, the
      fixture-driven run must emit at least one record whose
      `license.is_redistributable` is `True`, **and** at least one
      record with `License.UNKNOWN` (we want both paths covered).
- [ ] `tests/test_baseline_regression.py`: load
      `reports/baseline-*.json` (newest), re-run the fixture harness,
      and assert per-source `mean_quality` is within ±0.02 of the
      stored baseline. Any intentional improvement updates the
      baseline in the same PR.
- [ ] CI: add a `schema` job that runs these three tests in isolation
      and publishes a short JSON summary as an artifact.

### P1 — license-laundering defense (H2)

- [ ] Add `License.VERIFIED` boolean *field* on `VideoRecord`
      (`license_verified: bool = False`), plus an update to
      `db_structured.md`. Don't add a new enum value — keep the
      existing `License` enum as declared taxonomy and add verification
      as orthogonal metadata.
- [ ] `src/specint/quality/license_verify.py`: pure function
      `verify(record, html_fetcher) -> bool`, with `html_fetcher`
      injectable so unit tests pass a fixture HTML blob.
- [ ] Wire into each adapter's `parse`: if the upstream payload also
      contains an independent license hint (e.g. Wikimedia
      `extmetadata.LicenseShortName` *and* `extmetadata.UsageTerms`;
      Internet Archive `metadata.licenseurl` *and* `metadata.rights`),
      require both to agree before setting `license_verified=True`.
      For Common Crawl, defer the verification until a page re-fetch
      step (flag only; do not fetch in unit tests).
- [ ] Quality scorer: add `license_verified` component (weight 0.10,
      taken from `license_clean` 0.35 → 0.25). Document the shift in
      `metrics.py`.

### P1 — Web Data Commons Recipe adapter (H1)

- [ ] `src/specint/sources/wdc_recipe.py`: parse one N-Quads line (or
      lookup-file row) → `VideoRecord` when the Recipe entity has an
      embedded `VideoObject` with `contentUrl`/`embedUrl`. Defaults
      `license=UNKNOWN`, `license_verified=False`; the real license is
      determined by the page license verifier (P1 above).
- [ ] Ship an N-Quads fixture in `tests/fixtures/wdc_recipe/` sampled
      from WDC's "Recipe (sample)" download, with ≤50 rows and
      diverse PLD coverage (recipe blog, newspaper food column,
      cooking magazine).
- [ ] Add to compare harness; expect large `n_records`, small
      `n_license_clean` until P1 verifier is in. That delta is the
      informative result.

### P1 — Wikimedia category-recursion adapter (H1)

- [ ] Extend the existing Wikimedia adapter with
      `search_category(category_title, depth)` that uses
      `list=categorymembers` with `cmtype=file` and follows
      subcategories up to `depth`. Default seeds:
      `Category:Videos of cooking`, `Category:Videos of cooking
      recipes`, `Category:Videos of dish washing`, and the
      multilingual sibling category trees (depth=1 initially).
- [ ] Fixture: a `categorymembers` JSON response plus two `imageinfo`
      responses, including one `extmetadata` with
      `LicenseShortName=CC BY-SA 4.0` and `UsageTerms` that agrees,
      and one that *disagrees* (laundering check).

### P1 — cross-source dedup (H3)

- [ ] `src/specint/quality/dedup.py`: pure functions
      `title_shingle_minhash(record)` and `cluster_near_duplicates(records,
      threshold)`. Signature is title-normalized (lowercase, strip
      punctuation, Unicode NFKD) 5-gram MinHash OR duration within ±2%.
      Keep the representative with the highest `quality_score`.
- [ ] Add `n_unique_after_dedup` and `dedup_rate` columns to
      `BenchmarkResult` and `db_structured.md`.
- [ ] Unit tests with a hand-crafted fixture that places the same
      "Classic Carbonara" record on `archive_org`, a PeerTube
      instance, and a recipe blog (Common Crawl) — assert exactly one
      survives per cluster and that the Wikimedia copy wins on
      quality tiebreak.

### P2 — multilingual + spam defense (H4)

- [ ] `src/specint/quality/lang.py`: wrap `pycld3` or a vendored
      offline language-id model behind a `detect(text) -> str | None`
      interface. CI must still be offline; ship a tiny fixture-based
      stub for the unit test path.
- [ ] Add `_score_language_match` (if `query.language` set and
      `record.language` disagrees, score 0; else 1) and
      `_score_language_known` (1 if detectable, 0.5 if unknown) —
      documented weights, each 0.05.
- [ ] `src/specint/quality/host_reputation.py`: load a CC-BY-licensed
      hostlist (initial seed: a tiny vendored Tranco snapshot under
      `tests/fixtures/hosts/tranco-sample.csv`) and score a URL's
      effective registered domain. Only used as a soft prior; no
      domain is auto-blocked.

### P2 — procedural-ness prior (H1/procedural note)

- [ ] `src/specint/quality/procedural.py`: `procedural_density(record)`
      returns `(n_steps, avg_step_chars, imperative_verb_ratio)`.
      "Imperative verb ratio" is computed against a small vendored
      English imperative verb list (`chop`, `whisk`, `fold`, `simmer`,
      `sear`, `deglaze`, …) checked into `src/specint/quality/data/`.
- [ ] Add `_score_procedural_density` to `WEIGHTS` (0.10, taken from
      `text_density`).
- [ ] Fixture updates: at least one Common Crawl recipe fixture with
      a 6+ step `HowToStep` block, at least one with zero steps.

### P2 — CI / pre-commit hardening (user explicitly asked)

- [ ] Add a `--license-report` subcommand to the CLI: for each source,
      emit `{license -> count}` from the fixture run. Use the output
      as a **license budget** check in CI: `license_verified >= 0` is
      a floor that must not regress on the fixture corpus.
- [ ] `.github/workflows/ci.yml`: add a `benchmark-gate` job that
      runs `python -m specint compare --fixtures` and diffs
      `reports/ci-compare.json` against the newest
      `reports/baseline-*.json`; PR fails if per-source `mean_quality`
      regresses by more than 0.02. (Pairs with
      `tests/test_baseline_regression.py` as a last-mile guard.)
- [ ] Pre-commit: add `codespell` (fast, offline) and a local
      `check-record-id-format` hook that greps any added fixture for
      `"id": "..."` and asserts `"{source}:{native_id}"` format.
- [ ] Pre-commit: switch the `pytest-quick` hook from `pre-push` to
      `pre-commit` with `-x --ff -q -k "not slow"` to shorten the
      feedback loop (tests are already offline).

### P3 — generalization beyond cooking (do not skip; this is the frontier framing)

- [ ] Introduce `SourceQuery.domain: Literal["cooking", "surgery",
      "lab", "sports", "manufacturing", "general"] = "cooking"` and
      route per-domain seed terms + per-domain Wikimedia categories
      from a single YAML config under `src/specint/domains/`.
- [ ] Add one non-cooking smoke-test fixture (e.g. a Wikimedia
      `Category:Videos of chemistry experiments` stub) and assert the
      same harness runs end-to-end against it with
      `domain="lab"`. We are not committing to lab quality today; we
      are committing that the pipeline is *not* secretly cooking-only.

## Out of scope today (keep honest)

- Any actual video byte download. We remain URL+metadata until the
  verifier and dedup land.
- YouTube Data API live calls. We can **mirror the Common Pile
  curated-channels methodology** by checking in a CC-BY list of
  "cooking channels we have high confidence are original uploaders,"
  but the *API call* stays behind `SPECINT_RUN_INTEGRATION=1`.
- PeerTube federated instance discovery (still just the fixture list).
  Opening a new instance should require a plan entry.

## Risks the next reviewer should challenge

1. **Fixture bias.** Our fixtures are 8 rows today. Even after P0–P2,
   the harness measures our *fixtures*, not the internet. Any
   `mean_quality` claim must be read as "on this fixture set." The
   integration suite (live) remains the only real-world evidence.
2. **License enum vs verified flag collision.** Adding
   `license_verified` without changing `License.UNKNOWN` semantics
   means two sources of truth. Document in `db_structured.md` which
   wins: an unverified `CC_BY` record is *still redistributable in
   intent* but **not training-eligible** — we default to excluding it
   from the training corpus but keep it for provenance.
3. **Language ID on short titles.** Titles under ~15 characters have
   very noisy language detection. Our `_score_language_match`
   component must treat "unknown" as neutral, not penalized.
4. **MinHash on titles is not a video dedup.** It is a *candidate
   generator*. Any claim of true near-duplicate detection requires
   frame-level pHash, which is out of scope today (downloads required).
   Document that `n_unique_after_dedup` is a lower bound on true
   uniqueness.
5. **Curated-channel lists age.** A channel that was original-content
   in 2026-Q1 may be license-laundered by 2027. We need a
   `channel_last_verified_at` field and a CI check that flags any
   entry older than 180 days.
