# Panda-70M — 70M video/caption pairs via multi-teacher distillation

*CVPR 2024; Chen et al., Snap Research.*

## What it does

- Starts from 3.8M long videos in HD-VILA-100M, splits into
  **70.8M semantically-coherent clips** using shot detection +
  ImageBind similarity stitching.
- Runs **eight cross-modal teacher models** (image-caption,
  video-caption, VQA, subtitle-conditioned) to produce candidate
  captions per clip.
- Trains a **fine-grained retrieval model on 100K human-selected
  clips** to pick the best caption from the eight candidates. The
  retrieval model is then applied to the whole corpus.
- Distills the ensemble into a single student captioner (two-branch
  visual + textual) so future runs are cheap.

## Why it matters for us

- **Data flywheel design.** Panda-70M is a textbook example of the
  "quality-scored candidate pool → tiny human-labeled subset →
  learned selector" loop we should copy for both *source ranking*
  and *caption ranking*. Even our metadata-only Phase-0 scorer
  should be treated as a *first teacher*, replaceable by a learned
  ranker trained on ~1K human comparisons.
- **Compute realism.** Running 8 teachers is expensive but only
  needs to happen *once per clip*; the distilled student is what
  gets deployed. That mirrors our per-source `parse()` (offline,
  cheap, deterministic) vs per-source `search()` (network,
  expensive, cached).
- **Licensing cautionary tale.** Panda-70M is HD-VILA-100M-seeded,
  which means it inherits YouTube ToS ambiguity. Their license
  disclaimer literally says "users must follow the related license
  to use these video samples" — which is a shrug, not a policy.
  This is exactly the failure mode our AGENTS.md hard-constraint
  #1 rules out.

## How we'd use it here

- **Plan hook:** motivates a future "learned re-ranker" component
  in `quality/`, where the training signal is human comparisons
  logged from `python -m specint compare --interactive`.
- **Concrete follow-up (future PR):** a `src/specint/quality/
  ranker_train.py` that takes a CSV of `(record_id_a, record_id_b,
  preferred)` pairs and fits a small logistic regression over
  metadata features. Fixture-tested on 20 synthetic pairs.
- **Adversarial check:** compare mean-quality output from our
  current linear-weight scorer vs a learned ranker trained on 20
  synthetic pairs. If the ranker cannot beat the linear model on
  even a toy set, we have a specification bug in the features.
