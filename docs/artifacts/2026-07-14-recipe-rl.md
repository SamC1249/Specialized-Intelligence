# RECIPE-RL — verifier-cheaper-than-labeler for procedural planning

- **Paper**: RECIPE: Procedural Planning via Grounding in Instructional
  Video. arXiv:2605.19976 (2026).
- **Core asymmetry**: extracting clean step labels from noisy video is
  hard, but **verifying** whether a generated step sequence is
  temporally grounded in ASR transcripts is cheap (dot product against
  precomputed text embeddings) and scales to millions of videos.
- **Result**: turning the noisy corpus into a *verifier* for GRPO
  outperforms supervised fine-tuning on both annotated and
  pseudo-labeled plans; +7-8 pts macro-accuracy in-domain, up to
  +16 pts zero-shot.

## Why it matters for a data-collection system (not a training system)

The same asymmetry applies to **corpus curation**. It is expensive to
prove a video is high-quality; it is cheap to *verify* whether the
record is internally consistent. Cheap verifiers we can bolt in:

- **Title↔description alignment**: text-embed both, keep records whose
  cosine similarity exceeds a threshold. Rejects click-baity or
  auto-uploaded batch records.
- **Description↔recipe_steps alignment**: for Common Crawl records
  that carry both, verify the steps actually talk about the same dish.
- **Declared duration ↔ steps-per-minute plausibility**: e.g. 90-minute
  video with 3 steps is likely a compilation; 5-minute video with 40
  steps is likely a slideshow.
- **Author-page consistency**: same author's other records have
  matching language/keywords.

None require downloading video, all are cheap.

## Concrete hooks into this repo

1. Add a **`quality/verifier.py`** stub with pure-function verifiers
   returning `(passed: bool, score: float, reason: str)`.
2. Extend `BenchmarkResult` with `n_verifier_passed` per source.
3. Track a per-verifier "flake rate" — false-negative rate on a small
   hand-curated positive set held in `tests/fixtures/verifier_gold/`.
4. Publish `reports/verifier-<date>.json` alongside the baseline so we
   can watch verifiers drift as sources evolve.
