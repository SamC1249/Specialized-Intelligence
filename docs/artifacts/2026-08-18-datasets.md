# Artifact: Cooking-video dataset landscape (2026-08-18)

Capsule notes on public procedural / cooking video datasets, filtered by
whether we can **legally include them** in a training corpus under our
"no paid, no ToS, no paywall" constraint.

The columns:

- **Redist?**: are we allowed to redistribute the media (or frames)?
- **Commercial?**: does the licence allow commercial use?
- **Yield**: total hours or clip count.
- **Verdict**: `ALLOW` = ingest directly, `POINTER` = URL+metadata only,
 `DENY` = do not touch.

## Adversarial reading of the top three "ALLOW" academic datasets

### CaptainCook4D

- 94.5 h, 384 recordings, 5.3k step annotations, 10k action
 annotations. Egocentric.
- Apache-2.0. IRB-approved. All participants consented.
- **Why it matters for us:** it's the largest single legally-clean
 cooking corpus with **procedural error annotations**. Errors are
 exactly the kind of counterfactuals a world model must be able to
 predict (state changes that go wrong).
- **How to ingest:** publish a `CaptainCook4DSource(BaseSource)` that
 doesn't hit the network — the dataset ships as a downloadable
 tarball with a JSON annotation file. We just parse the JSON into
 `VideoRecord` and set `license = OTHER_FREE` with
 `license_url = https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0`.
- **Weakness for our purposes:** all 384 recordings are from a
 restricted geography (University of Florida participants). Diversity
 is limited.

### COM Kitchens

- Overhead-view, unedited, smartphone-captured.
- Dataset: 145 recordings.
- MIT for the code; dataset file distributed under a permissive
 licence (see `LICENSE` on the release).
- **Why it matters:** the *only* legally-clean **overhead** view
 corpus we're aware of. Complementary to CaptainCook4D (egocentric).
 Together they cover the two camera POVs that are useful for world
 modelling.
- **How to ingest:** as with CaptainCook4D, an offline JSON-parser
 adapter is sufficient. The dataset advertises `doi:10.32130/rdata.6.1`
 for the media.

### HowTo100M

- 136M clips, but **the media are YouTube URLs**, not frames. Only
 the transcripts + timestamped clip boundaries are Apache-2.0.
- **Why it matters:** pointer-only, but the transcripts are a
 huge unlabeled procedural-language corpus. Do NOT store any YouTube
 media. Feed transcripts to `text_density` scoring only.
- **How to ingest:** we parse the CSV of `(youtube_id, start, end,
 caption)` tuples and emit `VideoRecord` with
 `media_url = None`, `license = RESTRICTED` for the video and
 `OTHER_FREE` for the transcript (they are separable in our schema —
 we may need to extend `db_structured.md` to record dual-track
 licences).

## Adversarial reading of the two "DENY" datasets

### Ego4D

- 3670 hours — the biggest egocentric corpus available. Would be
 transformative for us.
- But: **requires a signed licence agreement per user**, credentials
 expire in 14 days, and redistribution is explicitly limited to
 "distributing … in a research publication(s), an academic
 publication(s), or any website through which such publication(s) is
 made available". That is a **research-only, publication-tied
 licence**, not an internet-scale-ingestion licence. Violates
 AGENTS.md constraint 1 ("no ToS violations", "no paywall scraping"
 — the EULA is a paywall in kind).
- **Verdict:** DENY. Do not add an Ego4D adapter. Do not download
 sample clips. Even the metadata index is EULA-gated.

### EPIC-KITCHENS

- 100 h of kitchen egocentric footage.
- CC BY-NC 4.0 = **no commercial use**. Frontier model training is a
 commercial use even for academic labs that later license models.
- **Verdict:** DENY.

## What to build next

1. Two offline adapters (`captain_cook4d.py`, `com_kitchens.py`).
 Feed by fixture; no network. This lifts our legally-clean cooking
 hours by ~2 orders of magnitude on Day-1 of ingestion (from
 ~50 h Commons/PeerTube to ~250 h with the academic datasets).
2. A `dual_license` extension to `VideoRecord` (separate media
 licence and transcript licence) so we can safely ingest HowTo100M
 transcripts.
3. A `CaptainCook4D` gold-set import — 384 recordings with error
 annotations gives us free supervision for a future
 `procedural_correctness` quality metric.

Nothing in this note requires a network fetch; adapters can be
prototyped from downloaded release tarballs and shipped with a fixture
subset for tests.
