# Adversarial-Agent Plan — 2026-09-18

> First adversarial cycle after the 2026-06-20 bootstrap. The seed plan
> asserted a specific hypothesis ("Wikimedia + Internet Archive + Common
> Crawl recipe long-tail Pareto-dominates any single source"); today's
> job is to pressure-test that claim, expose the *slice* problem
> (frontier labs win by re-weighting, not by better scrapers), and give
> `Coding-Agent` a prioritized backlog.

## Retrospective — what actually shipped

- Four adapters: `wikimedia`, `archive_org`, `peertube`, `common_crawl`.
- Metadata-only quality scorer with fixed weights and a first fixture
  baseline: `wikimedia 0.62 > peertube 0.57 > common_crawl 0.53 >
  archive_org 0.36`.
- Offline harness + `compare` CLI + GitHub Actions CI on 3.11/3.12.
- All tests pass offline; `SPECINT_RUN_INTEGRATION` gates live calls.

The baseline is a *floor*, not a ceiling: nothing enforces that new PRs
must beat it. That is Attack D1 below.

## Hypothesis under attack today

> **H2 (adversarial).** The 2026-06-20 comparison result is a
> comparability artefact, not a signal. Specifically:
>
> - PeerTube's yield is under-counted by ~2 orders of magnitude because
>   only 3 of ~2 095 instances are queried.
> - Cross-source overlap is uncounted, so `__total__` over-counts.
> - The quality scorer weights have never been validated against an
>   external popularity signal.
> - The largest legally-clean web-video corpus available — PleIAs
>   YouTube-Commons (2M+ CC-BY IDs) — is not ingested at all, despite
>   being explicitly permitted by `AGENTS.md`.
>
> A comparison harness that fixes any *one* of these will change the
> per-source ranking. If it does not, the harness itself is under-
> powered.

## Adversarial questions to attack today (H2)

1. **Slice > scraper.** V-JEPA 2.1 gained points by re-weighting an
   existing corpus (YT-1B ×3.8). Does our harness even *support*
   emitting a *weighted-mixture* row? Right now `__total__` is an
   un-weighted union. Ship a `mixture` row keyed by a weight vector.
2. **Coverage attack.** If we replace the 3-instance fanout with
   SepiaSearch, does mean quality per PeerTube record go up, down, or
   sideways? Prediction: goes down (more spam), yield goes up 20–100×;
   Pareto shifts.
3. **License-desert stress test.** In domains without any CC-BY
   corpus (surgery, laparoscopy), do we still emit useful rows, or do
   we silently emit zero? Prediction: adapters return zero and the
   harness emits `BenchmarkResult.empty`. That should be an explicit
   `DomainCoverageError` with a URL-only fallback row.
4. **Popularity validation.** Spearman ρ between our quality score and
   log(1 + Commons plays) — is ρ > 0.3? If not, our scorer is noise.
5. **Duplicate-load attack.** How many `__total__` records are
   cross-source duplicates? Prediction: on the current 4 fixtures, 0;
   on any live crawl, non-trivial. Ship canonical-URL dedup now to be
   ready.

## Risks the next reviewer should challenge

- **Popularity ≠ world-model utility.** Commons play counts skew toward
  short, sensationalist clips. Validating our scorer against plays could
  actively harm long-single-take yield. Mitigation: also correlate with
  runtime — if ρ(quality, plays) > ρ(quality, runtime) we are chasing
  the wrong signal.
- **YouTube-Commons license drift.** Uploaders can retroactively change
  a YouTube video's license from CC-BY to Standard. When we finally
  ingest, we must re-verify licenses at *use time*, not just at
  fixture-load time. Ship a `youtube_commons.reverify(record)` helper
  that hits the video's `licenseInfo` endpoint before training-eligible
  marking.
- **SepiaSearch spam risk.** Federating over 2 095 instances includes
  instances with adversarial SEO. Ship an instance-reputation table
  (start with just: min `n_users` per instance from the fediverse
  stats) before we bless the federated results.
