# Adversarial-Agent Plan — 2026-11-12

> First adversarial plan after the 2026-06-20 bootstrap. Five months of silence
> means every claim in the seed should be treated as unverified and every
> metric should be stress-tested before we add more sources.

## 0. Prior art the previous plan ignored

The seed plan picked four adapters (Wikimedia, Archive.org, PeerTube, Common
Crawl recipe JSON-LD) without reading the state of the art. In the interim,
two bodies of work have *directly* defined what "frontier video data for
world models" means. Any plan that does not place us relative to them is
guessing.

1. **NVIDIA Cosmos World Foundation Model Platform + Cosmos-Curator**
   (Agarwal et al., 2025/2026). Five-stage curation pipeline — *split →
   filter → annotate → dedupe → shard* — over ~20M hours of raw video.
   Filtering uses DOVER perceptual-quality + LAION aesthetic + motion score
   + text-overlay detection; dedup is embedding-cluster + cosine threshold;
   output is WebDataset shards. See
   `docs/artifacts/2026-11-12-cosmos-wfm.md`.
2. **HD-EPIC + Ego2World** (Bristol, 2025/2026). 41 h of egocentric kitchen
   video, 69 recipes, 263 annotations/min, lifted to 3-D with digital twins,
   then compiled into executable graph-transition worlds for *belief-state*
   evaluation. The target distribution we must learn to mimic is almost
   fully absent from our current legal-source yield. See
   `docs/artifacts/2026-11-12-hdepic-ego2world.md`.
3. **CaptainCook4D / Qualcomm Interactive Cooking** (2023 → 2026). Dense
   procedural annotations *including mistakes*. Our current `has_steps`
   quality component rewards presence of steps but ignores *procedural
   density* and *error annotations*, which the SOTA uses as training
   signal. See `docs/artifacts/2026-11-12-captaincook4d-mistakes.md`.
4. **VidaForge (GAIR, 2025)**. A fully open pipeline competing with
   Cosmos-Curator, using PDQ hashes + Cosmos-Embed for dedup. Confirms the
   open playbook. See `docs/artifacts/2026-11-12-vidaforge-pipeline.md`.

**Implication for us.** The current baseline (`reports/baseline-2026-06-20.json`,
total mean_quality 0.499, 8 records, 0.84 h) is a *toy*. It cannot be
Pareto-compared to any serious pipeline until we (a) measure in *hours per
crawl-day* of *license-clean, dedup-clean, procedurally-dense* video and
(b) extend to multi-hour, multi-source yield. Everything below attacks
those gaps.

## 1. Hypotheses under test (prioritised)

### H2 (highest leverage) — Common Crawl page-level license extractor

> Current adapter defaults every record to `License.UNKNOWN`, which is
> correct but *throws away* the entire source — `n_license_clean=0` in the
> 2026-06-20 baseline. The hypothesis: an HTML-level license extractor
> that looks for `<link rel="license">`, `cc:license` RDFa, OpenGraph
> `og:license`, and a small whitelist of CC-indicator footer patterns
> (e.g. `creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0`) can raise `n_license_clean`
> on a held-out crawl of 50 recipe pages from ≥0 to ≥15 **with zero false
> positives** on a hand-labelled validation set.

Attack: build a seed of 50 recipe HTML pages where we *know* the ground
truth (CC-licensed Wikibooks cookbook pages, OpenRecipes, Commons recipe
pages, and a long tail of clearly all-rights-reserved blog pages). Add a
`license_extraction` test with the labels. Fail CI if precision < 1.0.

### H3 — "Effective yield hours" dominates "mean quality"

> The current `BenchmarkResult.mean_quality` blends license-clean and
> license-dirty records, which inflates the number and hides the only
> metric we actually care about. The hypothesis: replacing the headline
> metric with
> `effective_yield_hours = Σ duration_s / 3600 over records where
>   license.is_redistributable AND quality_score >= 0.5 AND duration_s >= 15`
> changes the source ranking on the current fixtures. If it doesn't, the
> metric is worthless and we keep `mean_quality`. If it does, we commit
> both and gate future PRs on `effective_yield_hours` (never-regress) as
> well as `mean_quality` (informative).

Attack: compute both on the existing baseline fixtures, commit the diff
report under `reports/effective-yield-2026-11-12.json`, and mandate CI
checks the new metric via the regression gate (H7).

### H4 — Procedural-density quality component

> Cooking-specific bias is actually desired *for now*, but today's
> `has_steps` is binary. The hypothesis: a graded component
> `procedural_density = min(1, n_steps / 8) * min(1, mean_step_chars / 80)`
> better separates recipes-with-noise from recipes-with-real-instructions
> and does not regress the other sources (which have no steps and will
> score 0 on this component — *that is correct*).

Attack: re-weight to put 0.10 on `procedural_density` by stealing from
`has_steps` (0.15 → 0.05). Show the ranking of the Common Crawl recipe
fixture rises above PeerTube on *procedural signal* even while total
quality may drop (license is still gated at UNKNOWN until H2 lands).

### H5 — Cross-source deduplication is non-optional at scale

> Cosmos and VidaForge both treat dedup as a first-class stage because at
> scale the same clip reappears across uploaders. We have no dedup today.
> The hypothesis: a metadata-only near-duplicate detector keyed on
> `(normalized_title, declared_duration_s)` tuples with Jaro-Winkler ≥ 0.9
> and duration within ±2 s collapses ≥ 5 % of records in a seeded
> cross-source fixture *and* preserves at least 95 % of
> `effective_yield_hours`.

Attack: author a fixture with 3 duplicated recipe videos spread across
Archive.org and PeerTube (synthetic; this is realistic — many Blender
Institute videos are mirrored). Ship `specint.quality.dedupe` with
`collapse_near_duplicates(records)` and a unit test that asserts the
collapse rate.

### H6 — Domain-pluggable `QualityProfile`

> `AGENTS.md` says every system must generalise to surgery / lab / sports
> / manufacturing. Today's `WEIGHTS` are a global constant with a
> cooking-only `has_steps` term. The hypothesis: introducing a
> `QualityProfile(name, weights, extra_components)` abstraction with a
> `cooking` and a `generic_procedural` profile is a 1-day refactor that
> lets a surgery adapter later register `instrument_count` without
> forking `metrics.py`.

Attack: ship the dataclass + a `--profile` CLI flag; prove non-regression
by asserting `cooking` profile reproduces today's numbers exactly.

### H7 — Baseline-regression gate in CI

> `plan.md` says "future PRs must beat this on the same fixtures" but
> there is no enforcement. The hypothesis: a tiny gate script that
> re-runs `python -m specint compare --fixtures` in CI and diffs against
> `reports/baseline-2026-06-20.json` on `n_license_clean` (never shrink
> per source) and `mean_quality` (per-source may drop ≤ 2 % per PR, tagged
> `allow-quality-regression`) catches silent-rot PRs before they merge.

Attack: ship the gate script in `scripts/check_baseline_regression.py`,
wire it into `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, and verify locally it exits 0
today and would exit 1 if we artificially halved a per-source
`n_license_clean`. **Shipped in this PR.**

### H8 — License invariant is untested across adapters

> Every adapter has code paths where a record *could* leak a `media_url`
> for a non-redistributable license (easy regression on a copy-paste from
> `wikimedia.py` into a new adapter). The hypothesis: a cross-adapter
> property-based test that enumerates every fixture record and asserts
> `not record.license.is_redistributable ⇒ record.media_url is None`
> would have flagged a real regression during the June bootstrap if the
> adapters had been written in a different order.

Attack: ship `tests/test_license_invariants.py`. **Shipped in this PR.**

### H9 — Provenance `extractor_git` is a lie

> Every record today ships `extractor_git = "dev"`. `AGENTS.md` makes
> provenance a hard requirement; `dev` is not provenance. The hypothesis:
> a `specint.provenance.current_git_sha()` helper that reads
> `GIT_COMMIT` env var (CI) → `git rev-parse --short HEAD` (local, with a
> file-system fence to short-circuit on missing `.git`) → `"dev"`
> fallback, called from every adapter at construction time, fixes this
> without introducing a hard dep on `git` at runtime.

Attack: next plan. Captured here for traceability.

### H10 — Multilingual cooking is the unaddressed long tail

> Seed plan admits "English only" is an explicit follow-up. The
> hypothesis: adding seed terms for Spanish (`cocina`, `receta`), French
> (`cuisine`, `recette`), Japanese (`料理`, `レシピ`), and Hindi
> (`खाना बनाना`, `व्यंजन`), plus BCP-47 language filtering, grows
> Wikimedia and PeerTube yield by ≥ 3× without materially lowering
> quality.

Attack: next plan. Commons search is fine with non-ASCII, and PeerTube's
federated instances include `peertube.mastodon.host` (Spanish-heavy) and
`skeptikon.fr` (French-heavy). Ship a `multilingual_seeds.yaml` + a
per-language yield table.

### H11 — YouTube Data API CC listing adapter (metadata-only)

> `AGENTS.md` explicitly allows YouTube Data API with
> `videoLicense=creativeCommon`, but we skipped it. The hypothesis: the
> metadata-only yield (URL + title + duration, no media download, no
> frames) for a fixed daily quota beats Wikimedia and PeerTube combined
> on *cooking-tagged hours per API quota unit*. If true, it is the
> dominant *discovery* channel; if false, we have a numeric reason to
> ignore it. Either outcome is informative.

Attack: next plan. Requires a quota-aware mock + a fixture captured from
the API's documented JSON schema. **No live calls in CI**.

### H12 — Audio and ASR are discarded, but they are the cheapest procedural
### signal

> No current adapter touches audio. HD-VILA, HowTo100M, and Qualcomm
> Interactive Cooking all key off ASR transcripts. The hypothesis: for
> Wikimedia Ogg/WebM files and Archive.org movies, pulling the first
> N seconds of audio and running `faster-whisper tiny` gives us
> procedural keywords ("heat", "whisk", "fold") at ~5 % the storage cost
> of the video. Ship as a *post-metadata* enrichment step, gated behind a
> `--enrich-audio` flag so CI stays offline.

Attack: next plan.

## 6. Risks and "why am I lying to myself" check

- The research-artifact notes are summarised from web sources; the next
  Coding-Agent should verify citations before building on them.
- `effective_yield_hours` presumes `duration_s` is honest. Wikimedia's
  `imageinfo.duration` is honest; Common Crawl's JSON-LD duration is not.
  Any dedup/yield metric must track `duration_source ∈ {probed, declared,
  unknown}` eventually. Captured as future work.
- A license-regression gate that *never* allows a drop in
  `n_license_clean` per source is a sharp tool. If upstream Wikimedia
  removes a CC-BY-SA file, a routine fixture refresh would fail CI. The
  gate is therefore keyed off the **committed baseline fixture**, not
  live crawls; live-crawl regressions are explicitly out of scope.
