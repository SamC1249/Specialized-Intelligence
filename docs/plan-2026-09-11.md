## Hypotheses under test today

> **H2. Cross-source redundancy is non-trivial.** For legally
> permissive cooking video, ≥ 15% of records surfaced by the union of
> our four adapters are duplicates of a record already surfaced by
> another source (same permanent asset republished; same fingerprint
> under normalized-title + duration-bucket + author). A deterministic
> deduplicator is therefore a *first-class* member of the pipeline, not
> an afterthought.

> **H3. Procedural-density beats generic text-density for world-model
> yield.** A quality profile that rewards *cooking-verb density in
> title + description + steps* Pareto-dominates the v1 (generic
> text-length) profile on the same fixtures, measured by (a) higher
> mean quality on records that carry step supervision and (b) equal or
> better license-clean count. If this fails, we learn v1 was already
> capturing procedural signal indirectly through text-length.

> **H4. Metadata-only yield estimates are a useful pre-crawl signal.**
> Given only a `SourceQuery`, we can rank sources by projected
> license-clean hours per query without downloading any media. This
> lets us budget crawl-days against expected marginal yield rather than
> spending crawl on saturated sources.

## Risks the next reviewer should challenge

- Dedup fingerprint is deterministic but coarse; two genuinely
  different demos ("Chocolate cake, 5 min" from two teachers with the
  same duration) could collide. Mitigation: `author_slug` in the
  fingerprint; log collisions in `DedupReport.dropped`.
- Procedural verb list is English-only, which will bias v2 against
  non-English records. This is a known regression until the
  multilingual follow-up ships.
- `duplicate_rate` on today's tiny fixture set is a *demonstration*,
  not a population estimate. The number to trust is the trend line
  once we run against larger fixtures (or, later, live samples).
