## What has actually shipped so far

The `2026-06-20` bootstrap gave us four *metadata-only* adapters
(Wikimedia Commons, Internet Archive, PeerTube, Common Crawl recipe
JSON-LD), a metadata-only quality scorer, a comparison harness, and CI.
Baseline yield on the fixture set: **8 records / 3021.4 s / 6 of 8
license-clean**. That is a scaffolding, not a corpus.

## Where the pipeline is provably weak

I re-read the 2026 SolarWM paper ([arXiv:2609.02886](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.02886)),
Reka's world-model data-pipeline post, and the "100 PB video pipeline"
Staff+ system design write-up. Every one of them names the same failure
modes we do not yet address:

1. **No deduplication.** We can (and probably do) count the same clip
   multiple times — Common Crawl re-hosts the same recipe pages,
   PeerTube federates copies across instances, and Internet Archive
   often mirrors Commons uploads. SolarWM keeps a `selection.jsonl` of
   *rejection reasons* precisely because dedup is the largest single
   quality lever, and MLT-Dedup (arXiv:2606.12215) reports 91% online
   repetition removal at 90% precision.
2. **No cross-adapter license invariants enforced.** Individual adapters
   check licenses, but nothing in the harness *proves* that a
   `RESTRICTED`/`UNKNOWN` record never carries a `media_url`. One buggy
   PR erasing that guard is enough to poison the corpus. This is exactly
   what the SolarWM authors mean by "selection decisions kept as data".
3. **No yield metric.** We report `n_records` but not "hours per
   crawl-day per source", which is the number that decides where
   compute goes. Reka's blog is explicit: "first analyze each given
   video only a little bit"; we do not even estimate that.
4. **English-only seed terms.** Nothing in the pipeline is multilingual.
   Cooking is *the* domain where French/Japanese/Italian/Hindi/Mandarin
   long-tail is qualitatively different from English.
5. **No aesthetic / motion / OCR filters, no scene split.** NeMo Curator
   (`MotionFilterStage`, `ClipAestheticFilterStage`), Data-Juicer's
   `VideoMotionScoreFilter`, and PySceneDetect are all open source and
   fixture-testable offline. Our `quality/metrics.py` has *no* signal
   below the metadata layer, so it cannot rank two 1080p CC-BY cooking
   clips against each other.
6. **HowTo100M-style speech-caption misalignment is inherited by any
   ASR path we add later.** Only ~30% of narrations are visually
   alignable, ~15% timestamp-clean ([TAN, arXiv:2204.02968]). Any future
   caption pipeline must adopt the ShowHowTo /
   HowToCaption LLM-summarize-then-realign approach; we should design
   the schema now, not after we've built it.
7. **We are not attacking the highest-yield legal source we have not
   touched: Vimeo Creative Commons (V3C).** V3C1 alone is 7,475 videos
   / ~1,000 hours of CC-licensed Vimeo material, and the follow-on
   V3C2/V3C3 push it to 3,800 hours ([arXiv:1810.04401]). It is
   permission-form-gated for the video files themselves, but the
   *shot-boundary and metadata manifests* are public — a metadata-only
   adapter is fully within our legal allowlist.
8. **YouTube CC-BY is on the allowlist but we have no adapter.** The
   `videos.list?part=status` filter for `status.license ==
   "creativeCommon"` is explicitly permitted. Metadata-only (URL +
   attribution + license) is fair use; we should ship the schema wiring
   even before we spend an API key.

## Hypotheses under test (this iteration)

- **H2.** Adding a metadata-only near-dup detector will remove ≥15% of
  aggregate records on a realistic multi-source fixture *without*
  reducing `mean_quality`. If it does not, our sources are already
  disjoint and we should focus elsewhere.
- **H3.** A dedicated *cross-source license/media-URL invariant test*
  (a) currently passes, and (b) will catch a deliberate mutation. If
  (b) fails, our review discipline is a fiction — everything else on
  the plan is second-priority.
- **H4.** `yield_hours` at the same top-K cutoff differs by at least
  1000× across sources. If not, we can stop reasoning about "the best
  source" and start reasoning about "the best filter stack".
- **H5.** English-only seed terms leave >2× multiplier of
  qualitatively-different content on the table. Concrete test: expand
  seed terms with a small (checked-in, fixture-only) multilingual
  cooking-verb list and re-measure per-source `n_records`.

      detector on (normalized-URL, title-token Jaccard, duration
      bucket). Deterministic, offline, unit-tested.

      no redistributable field set on `RESTRICTED`/`UNKNOWN`, provenance

## Explicitly out of scope for this iteration

- Actual frame decoding, phash extraction, or GPU-dependent scoring.
  Metadata-first cascade must be exhausted before we spend the compute.
- New sources requiring auth or paid keys (YouTube API key, Vimeo API
  key) — schema wiring only, no live calls this iteration.
- Multilingual seed expansion beyond writing down the design; the
  measurement lives in the next plan.

## What the next Adversarial-Agent should attack

- Whether Jaccard-on-titles is actually load-bearing or a proxy for
  URL-normalization (run an ablation).
- Whether our `mean_quality` metric is Goodhart-safe as we start
  weighting license more heavily.
- Whether Common Crawl `Recipe.recipeInstructions` gives us
  step-annotated procedural supervision that beats HowTo100M ASR
  captions on `HTM-Align`-style benchmarks — the strongest theoretical
  argument that "legal cooking data" can *beat* the incumbent corpus.
